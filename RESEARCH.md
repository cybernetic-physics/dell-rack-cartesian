# Sources, reuse decisions, and modeling limits

Research performed 2026-10-14, before building the scene and connector.

## Hardware

- [Dell XE9680 rear service diagram](https://www.dell.com/support/manuals/en-us/poweredge-xe9680/xe9680_ism_pub/system-configurations-rear-view-for-poweredge-xe9680?guid=guid-05770ad0-8d47-4fb2-b91e-6963d3411995&lang=en-us): dedicated iDRAC9 Ethernet, LOM, OCP, USB, VGA, six PSUs, GPU fan cage. Use the H100/A100 configuration, not the Gaudi3 variant with rear OSFP ports.
- [Dell technical guide](https://www.delltechnologies.com/asset/en-us/products/servers/technical-support/poweredge-xe9680-technical-guide.pdf): 6U, 482 mm rack ears, 447/434 mm chassis width, 263.2 mm height. I/O wall is 855.5 mm behind front ears; GPU fan rear is 966 mm. Front reference: eight U.2 NVMe bays above PCIe expansion slots. Rear reference: five columns by two rows of GPU fans, four upper and two lower PSUs around recessed I/O.
- [Dell rack integration guidance](https://infohub.delltechnologies.com/en-sg/p/poweredge-xe9680-rack-integration/): approximately 1009 mm overall depth with bezel, 1200 mm rack compatibility. The main enclosure is a generic 600 x 1200 mm, 42U frame, not a certified Dell rack SKU.
- Dell reference images are extracted from the downloaded manual by `scripts/reference_images.py`, for local inspection only. They are not pasted onto the simulated servers. Dell retains rights to its documentation and trademarks.

## Reusable assets

- [NVIDIA datacenter pack](https://docs-prod.omniverse.nvidia.com/usd/latest/usd_content_samples/downloadable_packs.html): downloaded `Datacenter_NVD@10012.zip` (approximately 9.8 GB). The catalog contains 42U racks, NVIDIA SN2700 and other switches, DGX and generic server nodes, PDUs, patch panels, cable trays, and complete data halls. Existing rack, switch, and tray meshes are reused. DGX/generic server meshes are not relabeled as XE9680s. NVIDIA's asset-pack license applies; preserve the distributed license notices and do not present these as original assets.
- [Isaac Sim asset catalog](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/assets/usd_assets_overview.html): surveyed for USD environment/prop reuse. The dedicated datacenter pack provides better matching rack assets.
- Searched Dell manufacturer resources, public CAD/GrabCAD searches, GitHub and Omniverse for XE9680 CAD. No directly downloadable, clearly reusable XE9680 model was verified. The Dell-specific server panels are therefore original procedural geometry informed by the manual, not manufacturer CAD. Small fasteners, grille pitch, PSU detail and port placement are approximate.
- [RJ45-Newton-Insertion](https://github.com/123tthh/RJ45-Newton-Insertion), MIT, copyright 2026 Yx.L: inspected the active asset dependency tree and success criteria. Its Newton/MJWarp RL task and fixed robot gripper do not supply PhysX retention/removal. Reused its detailed socket mesh for passive Ethernet ports. `scripts/import_socket.py` corrects millimetre mesh coordinates despite a metre stage declaration, recenters and reorients the visual, and writes a self-contained USD. Original license is retained in `assets/connectors/`. The active socket uses a separately inspectable open collision cavity; the source project's oversized cavity is not adopted as a realistic tolerance.
- [KiCad libraries](https://www.kicad.org/libraries/license/index.html): evaluated RJ45 STEP alternatives (including RJHSE538X). CC-BY-SA 4.0 with library exception; not imported because the usable USD socket above avoids a CAD conversion dependency.

## Connector and cable simulation research

- [Newton contacts_rj45_plug](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/blob/main/newton/examples/contacts/example_contacts_rj45_plug.py), inspected 2026-10-14 PDT: separate dynamic plug/latch meshes, SDF contacts, a spring-loaded revolute latch joint, SolverVBD and a rod cable. Its latch is a rigid hinged body, not a deformable FEM clip. Adopted the mechanical principle in PhysX with original convex ramp/shoulder geometry and a physical pressing pad; no Newton source or mesh is copied, and the engine is not switched. Our passive spring stiffness matches the example's 0.15 N·m/rad; damping, limits and geometry are independently tuned for this scene. The original Newton cable setup and kinematic anchor/orientation correction are not adopted.
- [USD angular drive units](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/omni_physics/107.0/dev_guide/schemas/usdphysics.html): angular stiffness and damping use degrees, so SI per-radian spring constants are converted by multiplying by pi/180 when authored.
- [RJ45-Newton-Insertion](https://github.com/123tthh/RJ45-Newton-Insertion): source project itself documents incomplete reliable insertion policy. Selected the useful socket visual and the principle of validating depth, lateral position, orientation, velocity and dwell. Did not adopt its learned policy or claim its success rate.
- [InsertAnything](https://github.com/mzhsoul/InsertAnything): force-conditioned insertion in Isaac Lab, BSD-3-Clause. Relevant to later robot control, but no ready-made Ethernet latch/removal solution verified.
- [Efficient Online Learning of Contact Force Models for Connector Insertion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09190): research reference for contact-rich insertion; adding the learned force model is outside this manual-manipulation baseline.
- [IsaacSim-Cable-Creator](https://github.com/Galidos21/IsaacSim-Cable-Creator): inspected its capsule/D6 chain construction. No top-level license found in the downloaded checkout; no source copied into this implementation.
- [NVIDIA PhysX rope guidance](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-to-simulate-wires-with-a-physical-system/267131) and the installed `omni.physx.demos/.../RigidBodyRopeDemo.py`: selected the standard capsule chain with locked translation and damped bending. Implemented with native USD joints, no cable extension dependency.
- [PhysX joints](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.1.1/docs/Joints.html): D6 constraints and spring drives underpin manipulation and cable dynamics.
- [DeformX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22116): Cosserat-rod co-simulation is relevant for future calibrated cable mechanics, but introduces another solver and coupling layer. Current cable runs in the same PhysX scene as the plug and rack.

## Physical scope and approximations

Newton reference file revision inspected: `06c743786eeb6cd140e922c912b3e2172d74ead4` (latest file commit returned by GitHub on 2026-10-14 PDT). Its source is Apache-2.0; our implementation references its approach but does not redistribute its code/assets.

Physically simulated: dynamic plug, wall contact, keyed orientation obstruction, friction, gravity, force-limited hand drive, capsule cable motion and bending constraints, a passive spring-hinge clip, tooth/lip contact retention, and pressing-pad contact release. Manipulation commands change drive targets, not rigid-body poses. The cavity is open and assembled from separate colliders. No joint connects the plug to the socket or world for retention; measured seating/hook state only drives labels and controller guards.

Compliance approximation: the latch is one rigid link on a torsional spring rather than a continuum bending beam. It physically deflects and springs back, but does not model plastic strain, stress, fatigue or fracture. Copper contact springs, electrical continuity and calibrated breaking force are not simulated. Cable/clip stiffness, damping, mass and small connector clearances require experimental calibration before sim-to-real use. The prior hybrid implementation and its evidence are archived separately; they used a software-enabled fixed joint and are not evidence for the new latch. The new isolated regression explicitly checks contact deflection, spring return, a 5 N retention load, physical release, and negative controls (no pad force and no socket lip).

## Remote access evaluation

IsaacAutomator `devcontainer`, commit `1a708c60dc6f2eb6c00c774f729c109827fe80bd`, uses Ubuntu host installation and noVNC/x11vnc. Its connection and troubleshooting skills warn that noVNC may omit Vulkan viewport content. COS 129 cannot use its apt/systemd host roles directly. This implementation uses an isolated Ubuntu Isaac Sim container and the same noVNC/x11vnc approach, with loopback-only published ports and SSH encryption.

[NVIDIA streaming documentation](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/installation/manual_livestream_clients.html) describes native macOS and browser WebRTC clients. WebRTC needs UDP media routing and does not gain full connectivity from an ordinary TCP SSH forward. No public streaming/firewall endpoint was opened. noVNC plus recurring native RTX captures was selected for the existing VM. Actual browser screenshots, physics output and native captures are saved under `artifacts/`; a running web server alone is not counted as successful visualization.
