Recommendation and tradeoffs

Full hardware comparison ↗

C/A for the simulation showcase; A/A for the independent-axis prototype comparison. Both completed 22/22 valid trials. Their 70.52-point scores are a practical tie, not a statistically meaningful win. C/A is faster and lighter; A/A has fewer unresolved coupled-belt transmission assumptions. Neither is hardware-safety qualified.

Retain A/C as a lower-peak-load development alternative. Its specialized tool produced lower nominal cable-force peaks, but its 21/22 valid cycles and slower successful mean do not displace the leading reliability tier. B/A remains an option for one-sided mounting with available service space; B/C has lower observed reliability.

Scores retain the original 45/20/10/15/8/2 rubric; new force measurements and improved media have not been used to silently rescore it. All current power-loss screens have unresolved failures. How to read this laboratory

All videos come from recorded physical trajectories. Render-only replay never substitutes for the manipulation test. Each clip identifies its original version and quality limits; a diagnostic clip is not a finished photorealistic deliverable.

Saved environments stay on the existing VM under /workspace/rack_robotics/environments. Expand a version below its design for the exact run command and packaged evidence. The immutable shared rack and vendor assets are required: a scene file alone is not a portable environment.

Numbers describe this simulation, with estimated masses, idealized joints and simulator pose feedback. Nominal cable-reaction estimates have isolated calibration and full trace verification; they do not establish real-material strength. No electrical Ethernet traffic, physical reliability or certified rack load is claimed. Historical reload discrepancies remain visible.
